Who Regulates You

More than one agency has a say

There is no single Texas licence that covers everything a esthetician studio does. These are the bodies whose rules your documentation has to satisfy.

TDLR

Licensing, establishment rules, sanitation, and postings under 16 TAC Chapter 83 and Occupations Code Chapters 1602/1603.

Texas Medical Board

Where a service crosses into medical territory, it becomes a delegated medical procedure under 22 TAC Chapter 169 — not esthetics.

Federal OSHA

Hazard communication for the chemicals on your shelves, plus bloodborne pathogens once you have employees.

Why It Matters

What's actually at stake

Real, published maximums — not scare numbers. Every figure below is a statutory or regulatory cap, not a typical assessment.

Scope of practice is the sharp edge

Injections, medical needling, deep peels, and laser or IPL are outside an esthetician license. Performing them without proper physician delegation can be treated as unlicensed practice of medicine — a licensing problem, not a paperwork one.

Sanitation records are what inspectors read

TDLR assesses administrative penalties per its published sanction schedules, and disinfection practice is documented in logs — daily solution records, implement handling, and foot spa logs on the TDLR-approved form retained 60 days.

OSHA covers your chemical shelf

Federal OSHA maximums, effective January 15, 2026: $16,550 per serious violation and $165,514 per willful or repeated violation. Texas has no state OSHA plan for private employers, so these apply directly.

Figures are maximum penalties published by the agencies themselves and verified as of August 2026. Actual assessments vary widely with the facts. Read the full Texas Esthetician Studio compliance guide for the underlying rules.

FAQ

Questions esthetician studio owners ask

Can I do microneedling or chemical peels?

It depends on depth. Services must stay above the dermis; medical needling and deeper peels are delegated medical procedures requiring physician delegation under 22 TAC Chapter 169. The scope-of-practice guide in the Kit draws the line explicitly.

Do I need an establishment license if I rent a booth?

The establishment itself must be licensed, and if you rent booths you have to keep a renter list with names and license numbers producible to TDLR (§83.71). Both are covered.

How long do foot spa records have to be kept?

Cleaning and disinfection records go on the TDLR-approved form and are retained for 60 days (§83.108). The Kit includes the log.

Can I use any disinfectant?

It has to be EPA-registered as bactericidal, fungicidal, and virucidal and used per its label — and immersion solutions, including bleach, are mixed fresh daily. The disinfection SOP and daily log document both.

What has to be posted in the studio?

Current licenses plus the Chapter 83 health and safety rules and the human trafficking awareness sign (Occupations Code §1603.357). The postings pack has them ready to print.
