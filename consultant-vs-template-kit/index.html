Hiring a Texas compliance consultant vs. buying a template kit

Last updated August 2, 2026

We sell a template kit, so treat this as an interested party’s view — but a genuinely useful one, because the honest answer is that these two options solve different problems and there are real situations where the consultant is the right call. Here is how we’d think about it.

What each one actually is

A consultant sells judgment applied to your specific facts: they visit or interview, look at how you actually operate, and tell you what to change. A template kit sells the documents themselves: the policies, logs, and forms your industry is expected to keep, written against current rules and ready to fill in.

The mistake is assuming they compete. Most businesses that fail an inspection do so on missing documents, not on contested judgment calls — and documents are the cheap part to fix.

Which one you actually need

Choose a consultant (or an attorney) if…

  • You have already received a complaint, an inspection finding, or a board letter
  • Your ownership or physician-compensation structure is unusual — corporate practice of medicine questions need a lawyer, not a template
  • You are doing something the rules do not clearly address, or you operate across multiple states
  • You need someone to walk the space and observe how staff actually work

A template kit is enough if…

  • You are a standard operation in one of these four Texas industries
  • Your problem is “I don’t have the documents” rather than “I don’t know if this is legal”
  • You are opening, or preparing for a first inspection
  • You want the paperwork in place quickly and at a known cost
These are not mutually exclusive. A common sensible sequence is to put the standard documents in place first, then spend consultant or attorney time on the genuinely unusual questions — which is a much shorter, cheaper conversation once the routine paperwork already exists.

Start by finding out what you’re missing

Either way, the first step is the same: work out which documents your industry is expected to keep and which of them you actually have. That is free and takes about two minutes.
