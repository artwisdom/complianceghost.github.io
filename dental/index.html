Who Regulates You

More than one agency has a say

There is no single Texas license that covers everything a dental office does. These are the bodies whose rules your documentation has to satisfy.

Texas State Board of Dental Examiners

Infection control and sterilization standards enforced through 22 TAC §108.24, plus licensing and continuing education.

DSHS Radiation Control

Every x-ray unit — including panoramic and CBCT — must be registered, with written operating and safety procedures at each unit (25 TAC §§289.226, 289.232).

Federal OSHA

Bloodborne pathogens, safer-sharps evaluation, and hazard communication.

HHS & Texas HB 300

HIPAA Security Risk Analysis, business associate agreements, and Texas's 15-business-day records-request window.

Why It Matters

What's actually at stake

Real, published maximums — not scare numbers. Every figure below is a statutory or regulatory cap, not a typical assessment.

The Security Risk Analysis is the most-cited gap

A documented HIPAA Security Risk Analysis of your electronic patient data is required under 45 CFR Part 164 Subpart C — and it is routinely the first thing requested after a complaint or breach.

Privacy penalties are set by statute

Texas Health & Safety Code §181.201 sets civil penalties of up to $5,000 per negligent violation, $25,000 per knowing violation, and $250,000 for PHI used for financial gain, up to $1.5 million a year for a pattern. Federal HIPAA is capped at $2,190,294 per provision per year (effective January 28, 2026).

OSHA applies to every operatory

Federal OSHA maximums, effective January 15, 2026: $16,550 per serious violation and $165,514 per willful or repeated violation. Texas has no state OSHA plan for private employers, so these apply directly.

Figures are maximum penalties published by the agencies themselves and verified as of August 2026. Actual assessments vary widely with the facts. Read the full Texas Dental Office compliance guide for the underlying rules.

FAQ

Questions dental office owners ask

How often do we have to spore-test?

CDC guidance — which TSBDE enforces through 22 TAC §108.24 — is at least weekly biological monitoring, with results retained. The Kit includes the monitoring log.

Do our assistants need a separate x-ray certificate?

Any dental assistant who positions or exposes x-rays must hold a TSBDE radiology certificate. The staff credential log in the Kit tracks it alongside licenses and CE.

Does CBCT need to be registered?

Yes — panoramic and cone-beam units are registered with DSHS like any other x-ray machine, with fees current and written procedures available at the unit.

We did a risk analysis years ago. Is that enough?

A Security Risk Analysis is expected to be updated as your systems and practice change, not completed once. The workbook is built so the update is a short exercise rather than a restart.

How long do we keep patient records?

At least 5 years from the date of last treatment under 22 TAC §108.8; for a minor, until age 21 or 5 years, whichever is longer. The retention schedule covers both.
